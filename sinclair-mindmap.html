Can Ageing Be Reversed?

Harvard geneticist reveals the Information Theory of Aging, cellular reprogramming, and the supplement stack he actually takes.

DR. DAVID SINCLAIR — REVERSING AGEING
💾 The Information Theory of Ageing ▼
Ageing is a software problem, not hardware
DNA is the hardware — it stays largely intact. The epigenome (which genes get switched on or off) is the software. Ageing happens when the software becomes corrupted. The hardware backup still exists and can be reinstalled.
Cells lose their identity — "cellular amnesia"
Sirtuin proteins maintain cellular identity by regulating gene expression. When DNA breaks (from stress, UV, noise, etc.), sirtuins abandon their posts to repair damage. During repair they lose their position, and cells begin expressing the wrong genes — the root cause of ageing.
The yeast discovery that started it all
Sinclair discovered in yeast that cells age by losing their "mating type" identity — becoming sterile and confused. The same mechanism operates in human cells. This insight has driven 30 years of research.
Ageing is the root cause of most disease
Cancer, Alzheimer's, heart disease and blindness are not independent diseases — they are symptoms of the same underlying process: ageing. Reverse ageing and these diseases largely disappear. This reframes the entire target of medicine.
🧪 Gene Therapy & Age Reversal Research ▼
Cells reset 75% younger in 8 weeks
Using three specific genes (Yamanaka factors — Oct4, Sox2, Klf4), Sinclair's lab activated them in optic nerve cells for 6–8 weeks. Biological age markers reset to approximately 75% younger. This is not slowing ageing — it is reversing it.
Human trial for blindness reversal — pending FDA
The eye was chosen as the first target because it is safe and isolated. Gene therapy delivered to the optic nerve can restore vision lost to glaucoma and age-related damage. Human trials are submitted to the FDA. If approved and successful in 2026, this opens the door to whole-body age reversal.
Rejuvenation can be repeated — not a one-shot wonder
In mice, the eye has been rejuvenated at least twice with the same success. The backup copy of youthful epigenetic information appears to remain intact regardless of how many times it has been reset. This suggests the treatment is not a one-time fix.
Accelerated ageing mouse model — OSK proof
By activating a gene from slime mould, Sinclair's team created mice that aged rapidly. Activating the OSK genes (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4) in these mice reversed the accelerated ageing. This served as proof that the backup copy of youth exists and can be accessed.
Cancer connection — same information loss
Cancer cells express genes incorrectly — the same mechanism as aged cells. Sinclair's lab has used epigenetic reprogramming to slow and kill certain cancer cells. Cancer may be another manifestation of the information loss problem rather than a separate disease entirely.
🍽️ Diet, Fasting & Longevity ▼
Three meals a day may be shortening your life
Constant eating keeps mTOR (growth pathway) perpetually activated and prevents autophagy (cellular cleanup). The body never gets the signal to activate longevity genes. Sinclair skips breakfast and eats his first meal around noon — not for weight loss but for longevity signalling.
"Good stress" activates survival circuits
Fasting, cold exposure, heat (sauna), and intense exercise are all forms of hormetic stress — mild adversity that activates the body's defence and repair systems. These same systems are the ones that slow ageing. Comfort is the enemy of longevity at a biological level.
Plant-based eating — the simplest upgrade
Plant foods are rich in xenohormetics — molecules plants produce under stress that, when consumed, activate human longevity pathways (sirtuins, AMPK). Reducing animal protein also lowers mTOR activation. Sinclair is largely plant-based but not dogmatic about it.
Keto — short-term tool, not long-term strategy
Ketosis activates some beneficial pathways but long-term high animal-fat keto diets may accelerate ageing through other mechanisms. Sinclair sees it as a useful metabolic reset, not a lifestyle. Exogenous ketones offer some benefits without the dietary cost.
The 5 most impactful foods for lifespan
Sinclair highlights: broccoli and cruciferous vegetables (sulforaphane), olive oil (oleocanthal), matcha and green tea (EGCG), blueberries and dark berries (resveratrol precursors), and legumes (protein without mTOR spike). These are evidence-based, not trend-based.
💊 Sinclair's Supplement Stack ▼
NMN — NAD+ precursor he takes daily
NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) boosts NAD+ levels, which decline with age. NAD+ is essential for sirtuin activity — the very proteins that maintain cellular identity and slow ageing. Sinclair takes 1g daily in the morning. NR is a cheaper alternative with similar but slightly less potent effects.
Resveratrol — activates sirtuins
Resveratrol activates SIRT1, a key sirtuin, mimicking the effect of calorie restriction. Sinclair takes 1g daily with olive oil (fat dramatically increases absorption). The clinical evidence is debated but he takes it based on his own research and biological plausibility.
Metformin — the diabetes drug for longevity
Metformin activates AMPK (the cellular energy sensor) and reduces mTOR — mimicking the effects of fasting. Long-term diabetics taking metformin live longer than non-diabetics not taking it. Sinclair takes 500–1000mg daily. Caution: may blunt muscle gains if taken around exercise.
Vitamin D3 + K2, and low-dose aspirin
Most people are deficient in vitamin D, which affects thousands of gene expressions. K2 directs calcium to bones rather than arteries. Low-dose aspirin has evidence for cardiovascular and some cancer prevention benefits, though the risk-benefit calculation is individual.
Fewer supplements may be better
Sinclair has simplified his stack over time. He now avoids supplements that activate growth pathways (like branched-chain amino acids) because mTOR activation may accelerate ageing. The principle: activate longevity pathways (sirtuins, AMPK), suppress growth pathways (mTOR, IGF-1).
🔭 The Future — Living to 150 and Beyond ▼
Living to 80 might be the alarming outcome
Sinclair argues that accepting 80 as a natural lifespan is like accepting childhood death as natural — it is only "normal" because we have not yet solved it. He believes people alive today may routinely live to 120–150 with interventions becoming available in the 2030s–2040s.
2040 — medicines that reset biological age
Sinclair believes medicines that meaningfully reset biological age will be available by around 2040. The human blindness trial is the first step. If it succeeds, whole-body age reversal treatments for joints, organs, and brain tissue will follow rapidly.
The longevity escape velocity question
Ray Kurzweil's theory: if science can extend your life by more than one year per year, you escape death indefinitely. Sinclair is cautiously sceptical of the timeline but does not dismiss it — he says it is dangerous to bet against Kurzweil's predictions given his track record on AI.
Societal implications — meaning, purpose, retirement
A society where people live to 150 looks radically different. Multiple careers. Retirement in your 80s. Relationships that last a century. Sinclair believes longer lives create more meaning, not less — more time to pursue what matters. But social structures built around 80-year lifespans will need to be rebuilt.
Democratising longevity — the access question
Sinclair is emphatic that age-reversal treatments must not become a luxury for the wealthy. He advocates for making them as cheap and accessible as the flu vaccine. His concern: a world where the rich are biologically 40 and the poor are biologically 80 is a catastrophic social outcome.
🏃 Lifestyle Habits That Matter Most ▼
Exercise — the single most powerful intervention
No supplement matches the longevity effect of regular vigorous exercise. It activates AMPK, boosts NAD+, stimulates autophagy, and maintains mitochondrial health. Both resistance training (preserve muscle) and cardio (cardiovascular and metabolic health) are necessary — not optional.
Sauna and cold plunge — real or hype?
Both have meaningful evidence. Sauna activates heat shock proteins and mimics some cardiovascular exercise benefits. Cold exposure activates brown fat, norepinephrine, and stress resistance pathways. Sinclair does both but emphasises they augment — not replace — diet and exercise.
Avoid smoking, excess alcohol, and chronic stress
These cause DNA breaks — the very trigger that distracts sirtuins from their maintenance role. Chronic psychological stress is biologically damaging in the same way as UV radiation or toxins. Managing stress is not soft advice — it is mechanistically tied to the rate of epigenetic ageing.
Sleep — when the body repairs
Sleep deprivation accelerates every ageing marker Sinclair's lab measures. The brain's glymphatic system clears toxic waste during deep sleep. Sirtuins are most active during rest. Optimising sleep quality is one of the highest-leverage free interventions available.

Overview

Harvard Professor of Genetics David Sinclair joins Steven Bartlett for a 2.5-hour deep dive into one of science's most provocative frontiers: the reversal of biological ageing. Sinclair argues from 30 years of research that ageing is not an inevitable biological process but a treatable condition — one that his lab has already reversed in animal models and is preparing to reverse in humans for the first time.

1 — The Information Theory of Ageing

Sinclair's central thesis is that ageing is a software problem. DNA (the hardware) remains largely intact throughout life. What degrades is the epigenome — the system that tells each cell which genes to switch on and off. Think of it as the operating system corrupting while the hard drive stays intact.

The corrupting agent is DNA damage from everyday stressors: UV light, toxins, loud noises, chronic stress. When DNA breaks, sirtuin proteins — the guardians of cellular identity — abandon their regulatory posts to repair the damage. During repair, sirtuins lose their positions in the epigenome, and cells begin expressing the wrong genes. The result is cellular "amnesia" — cells that forget what they are and begin behaving like confused, aged versions of themselves.

The critical insight: the original backup copy of youthful epigenetic information appears to remain intact. This means rejuvenation is theoretically possible at any age — not by rewriting the DNA, but by restoring the software from the backup.

2 — Gene Therapy & The First Human Trials

Sinclair's lab has used three genes — Oct4, Sox2, and Klf4 (partial Yamanaka factors) — to reset the biological age of cells. Delivered via gene therapy to optic nerve cells and activated for 6–8 weeks, these genes reset biological age markers by approximately 75%. Crucially, the cells did not become cancerous or lose function — they simply became younger versions of themselves.

The first human application targets blindness. The eye was chosen because it is anatomically isolated, making gene therapy safer. Human trials to reverse age-related blindness and glaucoma are submitted for FDA approval. If successful, Sinclair believes the same approach will be applied to joints, organs, skin, and ultimately the brain — whole-body age reversal within two decades.

Remarkably, in mice the eye has been rejuvenated at least twice using the same approach — suggesting rejuvenation is repeatable, not a one-time intervention.

3 — Diet, Fasting & Lifestyle

Sinclair makes a mechanistic case for dietary interventions. The core principle: activate longevity pathways (sirtuins, AMPK), suppress growth pathways (mTOR, IGF-1). Fasting is the most powerful known activator of longevity genes — it triggers autophagy (cellular cleanup), boosts NAD+, and signals the body to prioritise repair over growth.

Sinclair skips breakfast, eating his first meal around noon. He is largely plant-based — not for ethical reasons but because plant foods are rich in xenohormetics, molecules that activate human longevity pathways. He is sceptical of long-term ketogenic diets, viewing them as a useful metabolic reset rather than a sustainable strategy.

Exercise remains the single most powerful longevity intervention — no supplement comes close. Sauna and cold exposure offer meaningful but supplementary benefits. Avoiding chronic stress, smoking, and excess alcohol is not soft lifestyle advice — it is mechanistically tied to the rate of epigenetic corruption.

4 — The Supplement Stack

Sinclair takes: NMN (1g daily, boosts NAD+), Resveratrol (1g daily with olive oil, activates sirtuins), Metformin (500–1000mg, activates AMPK), Vitamin D3 + K2, and low-dose aspirin. He has deliberately simplified his stack — removing supplements that activate mTOR, including branched-chain amino acids.

He is careful to note these are personal choices informed by his own research, not universal prescriptions. The evidence base for some (NMN, resveratrol) remains contested in humans even as the mechanisms are compelling.

5 — The Future of Human Lifespan

Sinclair believes medicines that meaningfully reset biological age will exist by around 2040. He estimates people alive today — particularly younger generations — will routinely live to 120–150. He is cautiously open to Ray Kurzweil's longevity escape velocity theory (the point at which science extends life faster than time passes) but doesn't commit to a timeline.

He is deeply concerned about access. A world where age-reversal treatments are available only to the wealthy would be catastrophic. He advocates for making them as cheap and widespread as vaccines. He also argues that longer lives create more meaning — more time to pursue what matters — not less.

01

Stop Eating Breakfast — Try Time-Restricted Eating

Skip breakfast and delay your first meal to noon at least 3–4 days per week. This triggers autophagy and longevity gene activation that three meals a day suppresses. Start with 16:8 (eat within an 8-hour window).

02

Treat Exercise as Non-Negotiable Medicine

Combine resistance training (preserve muscle mass, critical after 40) with Zone 2 cardio (metabolic health). Aim for at least 150 minutes per week. No supplement replaces this — not even close.

03

Shift Toward Plant-Heavy Eating

Increase cruciferous vegetables, olive oil, berries, matcha, and legumes. Reduce animal protein — not to zero, but enough to lower mTOR activation. These foods contain xenohormetics that directly activate longevity pathways.

04

Activate "Good Stress" Deliberately

Regular sauna (4x/week, 20 mins at 80°C+) and cold exposure activate heat shock proteins and stress-resilience pathways. These are longevity signals, not just wellness trends. Do both if you can.

05

Prioritise Sleep Above Almost Everything

Sleep deprivation accelerates every ageing marker. Sirtuins are most active during rest. The brain clears toxic waste during deep sleep. Treat sleep like a performance drug — optimise duration (7–9 hrs) and quality.

06

Consider NMN or NR to Boost NAD+

NAD+ is essential for sirtuin function and declines with age. NMN (500mg–1g daily) or NR are the most direct ways to restore it. Take in the morning. Evidence is promising though not yet definitive in humans at scale.

07

Minimise Chronic Stress — It's Biologically Destructive

Chronic psychological stress causes DNA breaks — the same mechanism as UV damage. This directly triggers sirtuin distraction and accelerated epigenetic ageing. Stress management is not soft — it is mechanistically critical.

08

Track Your Biological Age, Not Just Chronological Age

Epigenetic clock tests (like those from Sinclair's partner companies) can measure your biological age. Use it as a feedback tool. If your biological age is higher than your chronological age, the interventions above can reverse the gap.

09

Watch the Human Blindness Trial Results

The first FDA trial to reverse age-related blindness using gene therapy is pending approval. If it succeeds, it is the opening of a new era in medicine. Follow Sinclair's lab (lifespan.com) for updates — the implications extend far beyond eyesight.

10

Plan for a Much Longer Life Than You Expected

If Sinclair is right, people in their 30s–50s today may live to 120–150. That changes everything: financial planning, career trajectory, relationships, purpose. Start thinking about what you would do with an extra 40 years in good health.
