Collagen Peptides: What They Are & How They Work
▶
🧬What Is Collagen?▶
Collagen is the most abundant protein in the human body, making up roughly 30% of total protein content. It provides the structural scaffold for skin, joints, tendons, ligaments, and bones. Type I collagen dominates in skin — it forms dense fibres that give skin its tensile strength and smooth appearance. Type II is primarily found in cartilage, and Type III is abundant in skin alongside Type I.
📉Why Collagen Declines With Age▶
The body's ability to produce collagen declines at roughly 1% per year after age 25. By age 40, skin collagen density has measurably decreased; by age 60, it may be reduced by 30–40%. Contributing factors include UV exposure (which degrades existing collagen fibres), chronic inflammation, high sugar intake (which causes glycation — cross-linking that stiffens collagen fibres), and declining oestrogen in women after menopause.
💊How Collagen Peptides Differ from Whole Collagen▶
Collagen peptides (also called hydrolysed collagen) are collagen proteins broken down into shorter chains of amino acids through enzymatic hydrolysis. This process dramatically improves bioavailability — rather than being digested into random amino acids, specific bioactive peptides (dipeptides and tripeptides) survive digestion and are absorbed intact. These peptides then signal fibroblast cells in the dermis to produce new collagen, elastin, and hyaluronic acid.
🔬The Clinical Evidence Base▶
A 2025 study published in npj Aging demonstrated that a collagen amino acid composition supplement reduced participants' biological age by an average of 1.4 years over six months. Multiple independent randomised controlled trials have also shown oral collagen peptides improve skin hydration, increase elasticity, and reduce the appearance of wrinkles within 8–12 weeks of consistent daily supplementation.
⚗Key Amino Acids: Glycine, Proline, Hydroxyproline▶
Collagen peptides are uniquely rich in glycine, proline, and hydroxyproline — amino acids rarely found in high concentrations in other dietary proteins. Glycine is also a precursor to glutathione (the body's master antioxidant) and plays a role in sleep quality and metabolic regulation. Hydroxyproline tripeptides specifically signal fibroblasts to upregulate collagen synthesis in the dermis.
Biological Age vs Chronological Age
▶
⏰What Is Biological Age?▶
Biological age is a measure of how old your cells and tissues function, as distinct from your chronological age. It is typically measured using epigenetic clocks — algorithms that analyse DNA methylation patterns at specific sites across the genome. These patterns change in predictable ways as we age, and lifestyle interventions can meaningfully shift them in either direction.
🧪Epigenetic Clocks: How Biological Age Is Measured▶
Epigenetic clocks like Horvath's clock, PhenoAge, and GrimAge measure the degree of methylation at specific CpG sites across the genome. Younger biological age profiles correlate with better health outcomes, lower disease risk, and longer lifespan. The exciting finding in the 2025 collagen study was that a simple daily supplement produced a measurable shift in these clock readings within six months.
🔄Can You Reverse Biological Age?▶
Multiple interventions have now been shown to reduce biological age as measured by epigenetic clocks: caloric restriction, consistent exercise, quality sleep, stress management, and certain nutritional interventions including collagen peptides. DeLauer emphasises that biological age reversal is not theoretical — it is now measurable at the molecular level, though results depend on consistency and the combination of interventions used.
📊The Collagen–Biological Age Connection▶
The 2025 npj Aging study found that a collagen amino acid formulation reduced biological age by 1.4 years on average after six months — a clinically meaningful shift achieved through a single daily supplement. DeLauer contextualises this within the broader longevity framework: reducing biological age by even one to two years could translate to meaningfully lower risk of age-related disease over time.
Wrinkle Reduction & Skin Rejuvenation
▶
🪞How Wrinkles Form▶
Wrinkles form through a combination of collagen degradation, elastin breakdown, declining hyaluronic acid production, and repetitive facial muscle contractions. As the dermis thins with age, the skin loses its ability to bounce back from compression — creases deepen and become permanent. UV radiation accelerates this process by activating matrix metalloproteinase enzymes that break down collagen fibres in the dermis.
✨How Collagen Peptides Reduce Wrinkles▶
Oral collagen peptides reach the dermis intact, where they bind to fibroblast receptors and upregulate production of new collagen (Types I and III), elastin, and hyaluronic acid. Multiple RCTs show measurable improvements in skin elasticity and significant reductions in eye corner wrinkle depth after 8–12 weeks. The mechanism is not simply adding collagen but triggering the skin's own regenerative processes.
💧Skin Hydration & Hyaluronic Acid▶
Collagen peptide supplementation has been shown to increase skin hydration — partly through the direct effects of glycine and proline on the skin barrier, and partly through upregulation of hyaluronic acid synthase. Hyaluronic acid holds up to 1000 times its weight in water, and its production declines sharply with age. Higher dermal hydration plumps the skin, making wrinkles less visible and the skin surface more reflective (contributing to perceived brightness).
🌟Skin Brightening Mechanisms▶
Skin brightness is driven by both structural and biochemical factors. Increased hydration plumps the dermis and improves light reflection. Glycine as a glutathione precursor helps reduce oxidative stress and may attenuate UV-induced hyperpigmentation. Better collagen density reduces the irregular light scattering caused by degraded collagen networks — giving skin a more uniform, luminous appearance without topical cosmetics.
Skin Hydration & Elasticity Science
▶
🧴Why Topical Collagen Doesn't Work▶
Topical collagen creams are largely ineffective because collagen molecules are too large to penetrate the skin barrier — they sit on the surface and create a temporary film effect but do not reach the dermis where new collagen synthesis occurs. DeLauer contrasts this with oral collagen peptides, which are absorbed through the gut, circulate in the bloodstream, and are specifically taken up by fibroblasts in the dermis.
⚡Elastin: The Bounce-Back Protein▶
Elastin gives skin its elasticity — the ability to stretch and snap back. Elastin production essentially ceases after puberty, so the body relies on maintaining existing elastin fibres. Collagen peptide supplementation has been shown to upregulate both collagen and elastin synthesis in fibroblasts simultaneously, improving skin elasticity scores in clinical trials within 12 weeks of supplementation.
📐Measuring Skin Elasticity▶
Skin elasticity is measured using cutometers — devices that apply suction to skin and measure how quickly it rebounds. Studies using collagen peptide supplementation consistently show significant improvements in cutometer readings after 8–12 weeks. DeLauer notes that these measurements are objective and reproducible, making them reliable evidence of structural skin improvement rather than subjective perception.
🔋Optimal Dosing & Timing▶
Most research on skin outcomes uses 5–10g of hydrolysed collagen peptides per day. DeLauer notes that Vitamin C is essential for collagen synthesis — without adequate Vitamin C, the body cannot hydroxylate proline into hydroxyproline, a critical step in collagen formation. Taking collagen with a Vitamin C source (or a supplement containing both) maximises the biological yield from supplementation. Morning or post-exercise timing may improve uptake.
The Gut–Skin Axis
▶
🔗How Gut Health Affects Skin▶
The gut–skin axis describes the bidirectional communication between the gastrointestinal microbiome and skin health. A disrupted gut microbiome increases intestinal permeability, allowing inflammatory bacterial products to circulate systemically — triggering skin inflammation, accelerating collagen degradation, and worsening conditions like acne, eczema, and rosacea. Healing the gut often produces visible skin improvements.
🦠Gut Microbiome & Collagen Production▶
Beneficial gut bacteria produce short-chain fatty acids that reduce systemic inflammation — directly protecting collagen fibres from inflammatory degradation. Some species also produce enzymes that improve the bioavailability of ingested collagen peptides. DeLauer argues that optimising the gut microbiome (through fibre, fermented foods, and reduced UPFs) compounds the benefits of collagen supplementation.
🥗Dietary Foundations for Skin Health▶
Beyond collagen peptides, DeLauer highlights dietary patterns that preserve skin collagen: eating adequate protein (the amino acid pool from whole foods supports collagen synthesis), consuming polyphenol-rich foods (berries, dark chocolate, green tea) that inhibit collagen-degrading enzymes, and minimising dietary sugar (which glycates and stiffens collagen through the Maillard reaction, accelerating visible aging).
😴Sleep & Skin Regeneration▶
Deep sleep (slow-wave sleep) is when the body performs the majority of its collagen repair and synthesis. Growth hormone — released in pulses during deep sleep — directly stimulates fibroblast activity and collagen production. DeLauer emphasises that even optimal supplementation produces diminished returns without adequate sleep: 7–9 hours in a cool, dark environment is a non-negotiable foundation for skin regeneration.
Anti-Aging Lifestyle Stack
▶
🏋Exercise & Collagen Synthesis▶
Resistance training stimulates collagen synthesis in both skin and connective tissue by increasing mechanical tension on fibroblasts. Studies show that taking collagen peptides 30–60 minutes before exercise may further enhance collagen synthesis in tendons and skin by providing available amino acids during the post-exercise anabolic window. DeLauer advocates pairing supplementation with consistent strength training for maximal anti-aging results.
☀UV Protection as Priority One▶
UV radiation is the single largest environmental driver of skin aging — responsible for up to 80% of visible facial aging. UVA rays penetrate deep into the dermis and activate matrix metalloproteinases that destroy collagen and elastin fibres. DeLauer stresses that supplementation works best when combined with consistent daily SPF 30+ application, making sun protection the most cost-effective anti-aging intervention available.
🚫Collagen Destroyers to Eliminate▶
Several lifestyle factors actively accelerate collagen destruction: smoking (carbon monoxide and free radicals directly break down collagen fibres), excess sugar intake (glycation cross-links and stiffens collagen), chronic stress (cortisol degrades collagen and thins skin over time), and alcohol (dehydrates skin and generates oxidative stress that damages collagen networks). Reducing these factors has a compounding effect with supplementation.
🌊Hydration & Skin Barrier Function▶
Adequate water intake (typically 2–3 litres per day depending on body size and activity) maintains the turgor pressure that keeps skin plump and elastic. DeLauer notes that electrolytes — particularly sodium and potassium — are necessary for water to be effectively retained intracellularly. A well-hydrated skin barrier is more resistant to environmental damage and supports faster collagen repair after UV or mechanical stress.

Wrinkle Reduction: Mechanisms and Evidence

Wrinkles form as collagen and elastin break down with age, UV exposure, smoking, and sugar-induced glycation. The dermis thins, loses its structural scaffold, and can no longer support the skin surface uniformly. DeLauer explains that topical collagen creams cannot penetrate the skin barrier — the collagen molecule is simply too large. Oral collagen peptides bypass this problem by being absorbed through the gut, circulating in the blood, and being actively taken up by dermal fibroblasts. Multiple independent randomised controlled trials show significant reductions in wrinkle depth and improvements in skin elasticity after 8–12 weeks of 5–10g daily supplementation. The effect size is meaningful rather than cosmetic — cutometer measurements show objective improvements in skin biomechanics.

Skin Hydration, Hyaluronic Acid, and Brightness

Oral collagen peptides improve skin hydration through two mechanisms: the amino acids (especially glycine and proline) directly support the skin barrier, and collagen peptides upregulate hyaluronic acid synthase — the enzyme that produces hyaluronic acid in the dermis. Hyaluronic acid holds up to 1000 times its weight in water, and its production declines sharply with age. Greater dermal hydration plumps the skin visibly, making wrinkles less deep and the skin surface more reflective. Glycine also serves as a precursor to glutathione, the body's master antioxidant, potentially attenuating UV-induced hyperpigmentation and contributing to the brightening effect DeLauer describes.
