The Mechanism 4 ›
PPAR-α activation+
Pterostilbene is described as a PPAR-α activator. PPAR-α is a nuclear receptor that, once switched on, turns up genes involved in breaking down fat for fuel — it's the same receptor family targeted by some fibrate medications used to lower triglycerides.
Fatty acid β-oxidation+
Downstream of PPAR-α activation, cells increase β-oxidation — the process of shuttling fatty acids into mitochondria and burning them. This is the literal mechanism behind the video's "burns faster" claim rather than a vague metabolism-boost framing.
Liver fat and visceral fat link+
Because the liver is where a large share of PPAR-α activity plays out, reduced hepatic triglyceride content is the most direct measurable effect, and it correlates closely with visceral fat behavior since both respond to the same insulin and lipid-storage signals.
The insulin sensitivity angle+
Better fat oxidation and lower circulating triglycerides feed back into insulin sensitivity — cells that aren't swimming in excess lipid respond to insulin more efficiently, which is presented as the secondary reason visceral fat becomes easier to mobilize.
The Clinical Evidence 4 ›
The study DeLauer cites+
The video links a 2021 randomized, triple-blind, placebo-controlled trial (BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies) that tested cranberry supplementation in 94 adults with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) over six months.
The dose used in the trial+
Participants took a single 144mg cranberry capsule daily — equivalent to about 13g of dried cranberry fruit — alongside a modest calorie deficit and vitamin E, versus a matched placebo group on the same diet.
What actually improved+
The cranberry group saw significantly greater drops in total cholesterol, triglycerides, fasting insulin, and HOMA-IR (a marker of insulin resistance) than placebo, and significantly more of them moved down a full grade of liver steatosis on ultrasound.
What didn't move+
Liver enzymes (ALT, AST, ALP) improved in both groups but not significantly more in the cranberry group once adjusted for baseline — an honest limitation worth keeping in mind, since not every marker in the trial responded to the supplement.
