✨Collagen: The "1 Scoop" Explained▶
What Collagen Peptides Are▶
Collagen peptides are hydrolysed collagen — broken into smaller amino acid chains (primarily glycine, proline, and hydroxyproline) that are easily absorbed through the gut wall. Unlike intact collagen, these peptides can enter the bloodstream and signal fibroblasts in the dermis to upregulate native collagen production.
Why Collagen Declines With Age▶
After the mid-20s, the body produces approximately 1% less collagen per year. Cumulative UV exposure, high sugar intake (which causes glycation of collagen fibres), smoking, and chronic inflammation all accelerate this decline. By age 40, the skin's structural scaffolding has measurably thinned, causing visible wrinkles and reduced elasticity.
The Fibroblast Signalling Mechanism▶
Ingested collagen peptides — particularly the dipeptide prolyl-hydroxyproline (Pro-Hyp) — are detected by fibroblasts in the skin's dermis. These cells respond by increasing synthesis of Type I and Type III collagen, hyaluronic acid, and elastin. This makes oral collagen supplementation a true upstream signalling intervention, not just a protein source.
Clinical Evidence for Wrinkle Reduction▶
Multiple randomised controlled trials with 2.5–10g of hydrolysed collagen daily for 8–12 weeks show statistically significant improvements in skin hydration, elasticity, and wrinkle depth. A 2019 meta-analysis confirmed these effects across multiple populations, with benefits appearing as early as 4 weeks of consistent daily supplementation.

Glycine and Antioxidant Synthesis▶
Glycine, collagen's most abundant amino acid, is a precursor to glutathione — the body's master antioxidant. Higher glutathione levels reduce oxidative stress in skin cells, combating the reactive oxygen species that cause dullness, hyperpigmentation, and uneven tone. Supplementing collagen thus indirectly boosts the skin's intrinsic brightening chemistry.
Hyaluronic Acid Stimulation▶
Collagen peptide supplementation stimulates fibroblasts to co-produce hyaluronic acid, which binds up to 1000x its weight in water. Well-hydrated skin cells are plumper, light-scattering more uniformly, creating the luminous, "lit from within" appearance that tracks as skin brightness to the human eye and in imaging studies.
Melanin Modulation▶
Some research suggests collagen-derived peptides can modulate melanocyte activity — the cells responsible for melanin (pigment) production. Reduced oxidative stress in melanocytes leads to more uniform, regulated pigment distribution, reducing the appearance of age spots and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation that accumulate through sun and environmental damage.
Barrier Function Restoration▶
Collagen supplementation improves the integrity of the skin barrier — the outermost layer of cells that retains moisture and excludes irritants. A stronger barrier prevents transepidermal water loss (TEWL), which is the primary driver of dull, lacklustre skin. Improved barrier function is measurable within weeks using corneometric devices.
🕰️Biological Age Reversal — The Claims▶
What "Biological Age" Means▶
Biological age, unlike chronological age, measures how old your cells and tissues actually function. Tools like DNA methylation clocks (Horvath, GrimAge) quantify epigenetic ageing patterns. Thomas De Lauer references this concept to argue that collagen and associated lifestyle interventions can measurably shift these markers in a younger direction.
Collagen's Role in Cellular Senescence▶
Degraded extracellular matrix — of which collagen is the primary component — is now understood to promote cellular senescence (the "zombie cell" state where cells stop dividing but remain metabolically active, secreting pro-inflammatory compounds). Maintaining matrix integrity through collagen supplementation may reduce the senescent cell burden over time.
Systemic Anti-Inflammatory Effects▶
Glycine has demonstrated anti-inflammatory effects in multiple contexts — inhibiting NF-kB signalling and reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine output. Since chronic inflammation ("inflammageing") is now understood as the primary driver of biological ageing across all tissues, collagen's glycine load provides systemic benefits beyond skin that could measurably affect ageing biomarkers.
Nuance: Reversal vs. Slowing▶
De Lauer is careful to note that "reversal" in biological ageing terms refers to shifting measurable biomarkers — methylation clocks, inflammatory markers, skin structure — rather than literally reversing time. The science supports that these markers can move in a younger direction, but the magnitude and durability depend heavily on complementary lifestyle practices.
💊Optimising Collagen Supplementation▶
Dose and Type That Works▶
Research-backed dosing is 2.5–10g of hydrolysed collagen peptides daily. Type I collagen (from bovine or marine sources) is most studied for skin. Marine collagen has slightly smaller peptide sizes, potentially improving absorption. Grass-fed bovine collagen (such as that from Bubs Naturals) provides a clean source with predictable amino acid profiles.
Vitamin C as a Cofactor▶
Vitamin C is an essential cofactor for the enzymes that hydroxylate proline and lysine residues during collagen synthesis — without it, collagen cannot be properly formed in the body. De Lauer recommends pairing collagen supplementation with vitamin C (100–200mg) to ensure the body can actually utilise the ingested peptides for new collagen construction.
Timing and Absorption▶
Collagen can be consumed any time of day, but some research supports fasted or pre-exercise consumption. Taking it before exercise may increase collagen synthesis in connective tissue by driving blood flow to tendons and ligaments. For pure skin benefits, consistency of daily intake matters far more than specific timing windows.
What Blocks Collagen Effectiveness▶
High sugar diets accelerate collagen glycation — cross-linking collagen fibres in a way that makes them rigid and dysfunctional. Smoking depletes vitamin C and generates oxidative stress directly at the skin level. Excess alcohol impairs liver function and nutrient absorption. Addressing these blockers amplifies the benefit of supplementation significantly.
🏃Lifestyle Stack That Amplifies Results▶
Sleep as the Master Regulator▶
Growth hormone — which peaks during deep sleep — is the primary endogenous signal for collagen synthesis throughout the body. Chronic sleep deprivation not only reduces growth hormone output but elevates cortisol, which actively degrades collagen. De Lauer emphasises that no supplement compensates for consistently poor sleep when targeting skin and biological age.
Resistance Training and Collagen Synthesis▶
Resistance exercise creates mechanical stress on connective tissue, triggering collagen synthesis in muscles, tendons, and to some extent skin. When combined with collagen supplementation (pre-workout), multiple studies show enhanced tendon and ligament collagen deposition compared to supplementation or exercise alone — a clear synergistic effect.
Sun Protection as Preservation▶
UV radiation is the single largest driver of collagen degradation and skin biological ageing — responsible for up to 80% of visible facial ageing. SPF use is therefore the highest-leverage skin intervention per unit of effort. De Lauer frames supplementation and sun protection as complementary: build new collagen and stop the primary thing destroying it.
Fasting and Autophagy▶
Intermittent fasting, a cornerstone of De Lauer's broader framework, promotes autophagy — the cellular recycling process that clears damaged proteins and organelles. Autophagic clearance of glycated, cross-linked collagen fibres creates space for newly synthesised collagen to organise properly. This makes fasting a structural complement to collagen supplementation.

He explicitly distinguishes between in-vitro (cell culture), animal, and human studies, reserving strong recommendations for interventions with multiple human RCTs showing consistent effects.

The Compound Effect Argument▶
De Lauer's central thesis is not that collagen alone reverses ageing but that it is a high-leverage addition to a comprehensive protocol. Collagen + vitamin C + sleep + resistance training + sun protection + reduced sugar creates a compound effect where each element amplifies the others — the biological age reversal claim rests on this stack, not on any single intervention.
