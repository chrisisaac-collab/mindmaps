💊 Collagen Science & Supplementation ▶
What Collagen Actually Does +
Collagen is the most abundant protein in the human body, forming the structural scaffold of skin, joints, bones, and connective tissue. Type I collagen dominates skin architecture, providing tensile strength and elasticity. From our mid-twenties, collagen synthesis declines at roughly 1% per year, accelerating visible ageing.
Hydrolysed Collagen Peptides +
Hydrolysed collagen (collagen peptides) is broken down into short amino acid chains — primarily glycine, proline, and hydroxyproline — that are readily absorbed through the gut wall into circulation. These peptides have been shown to signal fibroblasts to upregulate collagen production, creating a genuine regenerative effect rather than mere topical masking.
Effective Dosing +
Clinical studies supporting skin and joint benefits consistently use 10–15g of hydrolysed collagen daily. De Lauer discusses Bubs Naturals collagen as a high-quality, grass-fed source. Consistency over 8–12 weeks is required before measurable skin changes appear, though some users report subjective changes earlier.
Marine vs. Bovine Collagen +
Marine collagen (from fish) is predominantly Type I — the skin-specific form — and has a smaller molecular weight that may improve bioavailability. Bovine collagen provides both Types I and III, making it useful for both skin and connective tissue. De Lauer favours bovine for comprehensive coverage and lower cost per dose.
✨ Skin Health & Wrinkle Reduction ▶
The Mechanism of Wrinkle Formation +
Wrinkles form when collagen and elastin fibres in the dermis break down, reducing the skin's ability to spring back after movement. UV radiation accelerates this through photo-oxidative damage, fragmenting existing collagen and suppressing new synthesis. Collagen supplementation addresses the internal structural deficit that topical creams cannot reach.
Clinical Evidence for Skin Improvement +
Randomised controlled trials using 10g daily hydrolysed collagen for 8–12 weeks consistently show significant improvements in skin elasticity, hydration, and wrinkle depth. One study measuring periorbital wrinkles showed a 20% reduction. These are objective dermatological measurements, not self-reported outcomes.
Skin Brightness & Luminosity +
Beyond wrinkles, collagen supplementation improves skin hydration through its influence on hyaluronic acid production. Better-hydrated skin scatters light more evenly, creating the "brightness" or luminosity effect. The glycine content of collagen also supports antioxidant glutathione synthesis, reducing oxidative dullness.
Synergy with Vitamin C +
Vitamin C is an essential cofactor in collagen synthesis — it hydroxylates proline and lysine residues required to form the triple helix structure of mature collagen. Taking collagen with vitamin C (50–100mg is sufficient) meaningfully increases the fraction of ingested peptides converted into new skin collagen.
🔄 Biological Age Reversal ▶
What Biological Age Measures +
Biological age differs from chronological age — it reflects the actual functional state of cells, organs, and tissues. It is now measurable through epigenetic clocks (like the Horvath or GrimAge clock), which read DNA methylation patterns to produce a biological age estimate that can be years younger or older than your birth certificate.
Collagen's Role in Ageing Biomarkers +
Declining collagen synthesis is both a cause and a consequence of accelerated biological ageing. Restoring adequate collagen through supplementation and diet supports the extracellular matrix — the structural environment in which cells live and communicate — which is increasingly recognised as central to longevity biology.
Glycine & Cellular Longevity +
Collagen is the richest dietary source of glycine, an amino acid with independent longevity properties. Glycine extends lifespan in animal models, supports mitochondrial function, reduces inflammation through inhibition of NF-κB, and restores circadian rhythm integrity. It is now considered a conditionally essential amino acid in ageing adults.
Measurable Change Timelines +
De Lauer discusses that biological age improvements from lifestyle interventions — including collagen, exercise, and sleep optimisation — can be measured within 3–6 months using commercial epigenetic testing. The convergence of multiple interventions produces non-linear improvements, suggesting synergistic rather than merely additive effects.
🧬 Cellular Mechanisms of Skin Ageing ▶
Fibroblast Signalling +
Collagen peptides absorbed from supplementation directly stimulate dermal fibroblasts to increase production of new collagen and hyaluronic acid. This fibroblast activation mechanism — confirmed in vitro and in vivo — means supplemental collagen acts as a signalling molecule, not merely as raw material for the body to use.
Oxidative Stress & Skin Damage +
Reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by UV exposure, pollution, and metabolic processes fragment existing collagen and activate MMP enzymes (matrix metalloproteinases) that degrade the extracellular matrix. Antioxidant nutrients — vitamin C, vitamin E, astaxanthin — work synergistically with collagen to neutralise this oxidative damage upstream.
Senescent Cells & Inflammation +
Senescent cells — cells that have permanently stopped dividing but resist apoptosis — accumulate in skin with age and secrete pro-inflammatory signals (the SASP). This chronic inflammation degrades collagen and prevents regeneration. Lifestyle interventions including fasting protocols can reduce senescent cell burden, improving the environment for collagen synthesis.
mTOR, Autophagy & Skin Renewal +
Periods of low mTOR signalling — achieved through intermittent fasting or caloric restriction — activate autophagy, the cellular clean-up process that removes damaged proteins and organelles including fragmented collagen. This creates space for new collagen synthesis and improves the overall quality of the dermal matrix.
🥗 Nutritional Strategy for Skin Longevity ▶
Protein Adequacy Matters +
Insufficient total dietary protein accelerates collagen loss, as the body preferentially cannibalises structural proteins during amino acid shortages. De Lauer recommends 0.7–1g of protein per pound of body weight daily — more than typical guidelines — with collagen peptides added on top rather than substituted for complete protein sources.
Anti-Inflammatory Eating Patterns +
Chronic dietary inflammation — driven by refined seed oils, high-glycaemic carbohydrates, and ultra-processed foods — elevates cortisol and inflammatory cytokines that directly degrade skin collagen. A Mediterranean-style eating pattern rich in omega-3s, polyphenols, and colourful vegetables creates the anti-inflammatory foundation that supplemental collagen requires.
Sugar & Advanced Glycation End Products +
Excess dietary sugar cross-links collagen fibres through a process called glycation, producing advanced glycation end products (AGEs) that make collagen stiff, brittle, and prone to breakdown. Reducing refined carbohydrates and sugars is arguably as important as supplementing collagen for preserving skin structural integrity.
Hydration & Skin Architecture +
Adequate hydration maintains the turgor and plumpness of the dermis. Hyaluronic acid — whose synthesis is supported by both collagen peptides and sufficient water intake — can hold up to 1,000 times its weight in water. Chronic mild dehydration exaggerates every wrinkle and dramatically accelerates the apparent rate of skin ageing.
⚡ Biohacking the Anti-Ageing Protocol ▶
Timing Collagen for Absorption +
Collagen peptides are best taken on an empty stomach or with a small vitamin C source to maximise uptake. Post-exercise is an optimal window — muscle protein synthesis pathways are upregulated and the increased blood flow to skin and connective tissue enhances collagen peptide delivery to target tissues.
Sleep as the Recovery Window +
Growth hormone — the primary driver of nocturnal collagen synthesis — is secreted in pulses during deep sleep. Taking collagen in the evening, combined with 7–9 hours of quality sleep, aligns supplementation with the body's peak collagen-building window. Poor sleep suppresses growth hormone and counteracts any supplementation benefit.
Red Light Therapy Synergy +
Low-level red and near-infrared light (630–850nm) directly stimulates fibroblast activity and ATP production in skin cells, independently increasing collagen synthesis. Combined with oral collagen peptide supplementation, the two interventions target collagen production from both cellular and substrate directions, potentially compounding outcomes.
Resistance Training for Skin Health +
Progressive resistance training increases growth hormone and IGF-1, both of which stimulate dermal collagen synthesis. Regular strength training has been shown to improve skin elasticity and dermal thickness measurably — independent of topical treatments or supplementation — making it one of the most powerful and underutilised anti-ageing interventions available.

Thomas De Lauer builds this episode around a central argument: that daily supplementation with hydrolysed collagen peptides produces measurable improvements in skin quality, wrinkle depth, and biological age markers — and that the mechanism is far more sophisticated than simple nutritional replenishment. When collagen peptides are absorbed from the gut, they don't just provide amino acid building blocks. They act as biological signals, directly stimulating dermal fibroblasts to upregulate their own collagen and hyaluronic acid synthesis. This makes supplemental collagen a signalling intervention as much as a nutritional one, with effects that compound over weeks and months.

Clinical research consistently supports this. Skin Ageing at the Cellular Level

Visible skin ageing is the surface expression of deeper cellular events. As collagen and elastin production decline from the mid-twenties, the dermis loses its structural integrity — skin becomes thinner, less elastic, and more prone to the creasing that settles into permanent wrinkles. UV radiation accelerates this by activating matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) that actively degrade existing collagen, while simultaneously suppressing new synthesis. Reactive oxygen species from pollution and metabolic processes add a further layer of oxidative damage that fragments the extracellular matrix.

Senescent cells accumulate in ageing skin and secrete a cocktail of pro-inflammatory signals that perpetuate this degradation. Understanding these mechanisms points toward the intervention stack De Lauer advocates: collagen supplementation to replace and signal new synthesis, antioxidant nutrition to reduce oxidative damage upstream, and lifestyle practices that clear senescent cells and reduce chronic inflammation at its root.

Biological Age and Longevity

De Lauer frames skin quality not just as aesthetics but as a longevity metric. Biological age — distinct from chronological age — is now measurable through epigenetic clocks that read DNA methylation patterns. Interventions that reduce biological age typically improve skin quality simultaneously, because both reflect the same underlying cellular health. Glycine, the dominant amino acid in collagen, has independent longevity properties: it extends lifespan in animal models, supports mitochondrial function, reduces NF-κB-driven inflammation, and restores circadian rhythm integrity — making collagen supplementation a longevity intervention as much as a cosmetic one.

Nutritional Strategy for Skin

De Lauer is emphatic that supplemental collagen cannot compensate for a fundamentally inflammatory diet. Refined carbohydrates drive glycation — the cross-linking of collagen fibres by sugar molecules — that makes skin stiff, brittle, and prone to breakdown. Dietary seed oils and ultra-processed foods elevate circulating inflammatory markers that degrade the dermal matrix as effectively as sun damage. The nutritional foundation must be anti-inflammatory: adequate total protein, omega-3-rich fats, colourful polyphenol-rich vegetables, and consistent hydration to maintain dermal turgor through hyaluronic acid function.

The Optimised Protocol

De Lauer's practical recommendation converges multiple interventions: 10–15g hydrolysed collagen daily with vitamin C for synthesis support; post-exercise or evening timing aligned with growth hormone secretion; an anti-inflammatory whole-food diet with minimal refined sugar; resistance training to drive endogenous growth hormone; quality sleep for nocturnal collagen synthesis; and optionally, red light therapy to directly stimulate fibroblast activity. The convergence of these interventions produces measurable changes in skin quality and biological age markers within 3–6 months of consistent implementation.
