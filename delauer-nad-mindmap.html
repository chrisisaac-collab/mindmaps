Why NAD+ Declines With Age
▶
The 50% age-related drop ▶
NAD+ levels fall by roughly half between age 20 and 60. DeLauer uses this decline as the central justification for the video: if a molecule this important is disappearing on a predictable curve, replenishing it becomes a maintenance issue rather than an optional upgrade.
CD38 and PARP consumption ▶
NAD+ isn't just under-produced with age — it's actively consumed faster by enzymes like CD38 and the PARP family. This dual pressure (less made, more used) is why simply "eating better" rarely restores youthful NAD+ levels on its own.
Downstream consequences ▶
A shrinking NAD+ pool doesn't stay isolated — it ripples into mitochondrial energy output, DNA repair capacity, and metabolic regulation simultaneously. DeLauer frames this as why NAD+ support can feel like it touches "everything" rather than one narrow symptom.
Why precursors alone can fall short ▶
Popular precursors like NR and NMN do raise circulating NAD+, but they're subject to rapid consumption by CD38/PARPs and the body's own homeostatic feedback loops that cap how high levels climb. This is the setup DeLauer uses to argue for delivering NAD+ itself, rather than relying on the body to convert a precursor.
How Liposomal Delivery Works
▶
The bioavailability gap ▶
Standard oral NAD+ or precursor capsules are estimated to reach only about 10–20% effective absorption, largely destroyed in the gut before they're useful. Liposomal formulations are marketed as pushing that figure toward 60%, which is the core selling point DeLauer builds the episode around.
Phospholipid protection ▶
Liposomes are tiny spheres built from a phosphatidylcholine bilayer — the same material that makes up human cell membranes. Wrapping NAD+ inside this shell shields it from stomach acid and digestive enzymes so more of the molecule survives intact until it reaches circulation.
Direct delivery vs. precursor conversion ▶
Rather than supplying a precursor and hoping the body's enzymatic machinery converts it efficiently, liposomal NAD+ delivers the finished molecule directly. DeLauer positions this as removing a conversion bottleneck that limits how much precursor-based supplementation actually raises NAD+.
Why the liquid format matters ▶
A liquid liposomal suspension increases surface area contact in the mouth and gut compared to a capsule, and some absorption is believed to occur through lymphatic uptake that partially bypasses first-pass liver metabolism. This is presented as another reason the liquid delivery outperforms pills.
Mitochondria & Cellular Energy
▶
NAD+ as an electron carrier ▶
NAD+ shuttles electrons through the mitochondrial electron transport chain, a core step in producing ATP — the body's basic energy currency. Without an adequate NAD+ pool, this chain runs less efficiently, which DeLauer ties directly to the fatigue many people notice as they age.
Fighting age-related fatigue ▶
Restoring NAD+ availability is framed as supporting mitochondrial respiration broadly, rather than acting as a stimulant. The pitch is that it addresses a root cause of low cellular energy output instead of masking tiredness the way caffeine does.
Positioned upstream of other energy supplements ▶
DeLauer contrasts NAD+ with supplements like CoQ10 or creatine, arguing those support specific downstream energy processes while NAD+ acts further upstream as a rate-limiting cofactor for hundreds of enzymatic reactions, including energy metabolism.
A complement to, not a replacement for, diet ▶
Even while pitching the product, the mitochondrial argument rests on NAD+ being a cofactor the body already uses constantly — meaning sleep, training load, and whole-food nutrition still set the ceiling on how much a supplement alone can move the needle.
DNA Repair & PARP Enzymes
▶
PARP1's outsized NAD+ appetite ▶
When DNA is damaged, the enzyme PARP1 activates and can consume up to 80% of the nuclear NAD+ pool to fuel repair. That single number illustrates how much cellular "budget" genomic maintenance can claim when NAD+ is scarce.
The repair-depletion cycle ▶
DNA damage triggers PARP activation, which consumes NAD+; but NAD+ depletion has been shown to reduce DNA repair rates by up to 40%, creating a feedback loop where the very process meant to fix damage is starved of its fuel. DeLauer uses this to argue that low NAD+ compounds genomic aging rather than just slowing it.
Genomic stability as an aging lever ▶
Accumulated, unrepaired DNA damage is one of the recognized hallmarks of cellular aging. Framing NAD+ support around PARP function ties the supplement to a mechanism deeper than "energy" — genomic maintenance itself.
Balancing repair demand with sirtuin demand ▶
PARPs and sirtuins draw from the same NAD+ pool, so periods of high DNA-damage load (intense UV exposure, heavy training, illness) can starve sirtuin activity of substrate. This sets up the next branch's argument for keeping NAD+ levels high enough to fund both processes at once.
Sirtuins & Longevity Pathways
▶
Sirtuins need NAD+ as substrate ▶
Sirtuins such as SIRT1 are NAD+-dependent deacetylases — they cannot function without NAD+ as a direct co-substrate. Practically, this means no sirtuin activator (including resveratrol) can do anything in a cell whose NAD+ pool is already depleted.
Resveratrol synergy ▶
Resveratrol works as an allosteric activator of SIRT1, increasing the enzyme's catalytic efficiency for whatever NAD+ concentration is available — it amplifies the effect of existing NAD+ rather than replacing the need for it. DeLauer uses this to make the case that NAD+ and sirtuin activators are complementary, not redundant, supplements.
Cell viability and senescence ▶
Sirtuin activity is linked to reduced markers of cellular senescence and improved cell viability under stress. This is part of the broader "healthy aging" framing rather than a claim about lifespan extension in humans, where evidence remains preliminary.
The "master switch" framing ▶
Sirtuins are often described as a master regulatory switch connecting metabolism, inflammation, and aging pathways. It's a useful mental model, but worth treating as a simplification — human longevity trials on sirtuin pathways are still limited compared to the mechanistic cell and animal data this framing leans on.
