Nitric oxide production declines with age
The endothelium — the thin lining inside every blood vessel — loses its capacity to produce nitric oxide as we get older. Less nitric oxide means vessels lose flexibility and struggle to relax and dilate properly.
The hidden cause of fatigue and brain fog
Li connects declining nitric oxide directly to everyday symptoms people rarely trace back to circulation: persistent fatigue, brain fog, and slower physical recovery, all downstream of reduced blood flow and oxygen delivery.

Because every organ depends on blood supply, the health of the vascular system has outsized influence on brain health, heart health, muscle performance, and metabolism — not just cardiovascular risk narrowly defined.

The Vascular Aging Story

Dr. William Li opens by challenging the common assumption that aging is first visible in the skin. He argues the more consequential changes happen years earlier, inside the blood vessels, where nitric oxide production from the endothelial lining gradually declines with age. Reduced nitric oxide leaves vessels less able to relax and dilate, which Li links to everyday symptoms rarely traced back to circulation: fatigue, brain fog, and slower physical recovery. Because every organ depends on blood supply, this vascular resilience has outsized influence on brain, heart, muscle, and metabolic health, and chronic low-grade inflammation compounds the damage over time. This framing sets up the rest of the video as a tour of specific foods that intervene on these mechanisms.

Blueberries — Anthocyanin Power

Blueberries owe their effect to anthocyanins, the pigments responsible for their deep color, which signal the endothelial lining to increase nitric oxide output. Li recommends roughly one cup daily, noting that fresh and frozen berries work equally well since freezing doesn't meaningfully reduce anthocyanin content. The same compounds also act as antioxidants, giving blueberries a combined effect of stimulating nitric oxide while reducing the oxidative stress that damages vessel walls over time.

Dark Chocolate — Stem Cell Repair

Cacao's proanthocyanidins work through a different mechanism than the antioxidant pathway: they stimulate stem cells originating in bone marrow to travel to and help repair damaged sections of vessel wall. Li is specific about needing 70% cacao or higher, since lower-percentage chocolate dilutes the active flavanol content with sugar and fat, and recommends a modest two to three squares daily. Coffee is discussed in the same segment as a complementary nitric-oxide-supporting drink rather than as its own topic.

Beets & Beet Juice — The Nitrate Pipeline

Beets work differently again: rather than stimulating the body's own nitric oxide machinery, they supply dietary nitrate directly, which bacteria on the tongue convert into nitrite and, eventually, nitric oxide. Li points to about one cup of roasted beets or beet juice as an effective dose, and flags a practical caution: because the conversion depends on a healthy population of oral bacteria, harsh antibacterial mouthwash can undercut the entire pathway.

Barley — Fiber, Cholesterol & Gut Microbiome

Barley's contribution runs through the gut rather than acting directly on blood vessels. Its beta-glucan fiber binds cholesterol in digestion, helping to lower circulating LDL, while gut bacteria ferment the same fiber into short-chain fatty acids with a systemic anti-inflammatory effect. Li ties this back to a theme running through the video: gut health, inflammation, and cardiovascular health are connected systems rather than separate concerns, and barley is easy to work into soups and grain bowls without any special preparation.

Apples & Coffee — Skin Compounds and Angiogenesis

Apples close out the food list with two compounds working together: ursolic acid, concentrated in the skin, and quercetin, found through the flesh, both of which reduce oxidative stress and stimulate angiogenesis, the growth of new blood vessels. Because the key compound lives in the skin, Li recommends organic apples so the skin can be eaten without pesticide concerns. He closes the video by tying all six foods back to a single idea: no single food reverses aging or prevents disease on its own, but consistently activating the body's internal health defenses through everyday food choices supports circulation and longevity from the inside out.
